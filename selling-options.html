Compare the route, the work and the expected proceeds

Start with your goal: the highest potential price, less preparation, or a move that fits your schedule. Compare written estimates using the same assumptions. Expected proceeds equal the sale price minus applicable payoffs, selling costs, repair spending and ownership costs while you wait. An estimate is not a guaranteed closing amount.

A direct sale to a home buyer

A direct sale starts with a buyer reviewing the property and discussing purchase terms. This may suit an owner who prefers a private process or wants to avoid managing a renovation. Ask about the proposed price, inspections, closing costs and timing. A direct property review does not guarantee a cash offer or an agreed closing date.

A traditional listing or sale by owner

A listing can introduce the property to a wider group of potential buyers. Discuss preparation, showings, marketing and costs with the listing agent. A sale by owner puts more of that coordination with you. If you are selling without a realtor, consider what professional help you want for the agreement and closing process. The best route depends on your property and priorities.

Repairs first or a sale in current condition

Before choosing to renovate, compare the work involved with your time and budget. A higher potential sale price does not automatically mean higher net proceeds after repairs, carrying costs and transaction costs. Use the resources below to explore the situation that fits your property, then compare written terms before making a decision.
